Help your teen start a small local business

Start with a service your teen enjoys and a family you know. This guide works for a small business run from home or in your neighbourhood, whether you choose pet care, babysitting, lawn mowing, car washing or something else. We’ll follow Alice’s Pet Pals as the worked example. Use the same steps to plan your own service and practise your first booking together.

Before sharing a flyer, agree who handles enquiries, who attends the first visit, and when a parent or carer will step in.

First, check what you need before taking bookings

Before putting up a flyer, publishing a website or accepting work, a parent or carer should check the rules and insurance for the specific service, the teen’s age and where the work will happen. Do these checks even if you’re starting with neighbours or running the business from home.

  1. Check approvals, licences and permits. Search ABLIS using your activity and location. If any requirements are unclear, ask your local council or the regulator listed in the results. Describe what the teen will do and where, including whether customers will visit your home. Australian Government guide to home-based businesses
  2. Check the rules for your teen’s age and role. Tell the relevant state or territory authority whether your teen will run the business or work in a parent’s business. Ask which rules apply to that arrangement, including permitted tasks, hours and supervision. Australian Government guide to starting a business when you’re under 18
  3. Confirm insurance directly. Tell an insurer or broker the teen’s age, who runs the business, what jobs the teen will do, where they’ll work and how they’ll be supervised. Ask what cover is needed, including whether public liability is appropriate, and get written confirmation of what the policy covers and excludes. Check with your home insurer if you’ll run any part of the business from home. Australian Government guide to business insurance

Keep a note of who you spoke to, what they confirmed and what’s still outstanding. You can plan and practise with fictional details while you wait. Resolve any outstanding approvals or insurance questions before launch.

2. Build the public enquiry form

Create a blank Google Form titled [Business name] enquiry. Say that a parent will review the request and confirm availability. Ask for the adult contact’s name, one contact method, suburb, requested service and preferred dates. Add one question that helps you size up the job. Alice’s Pet Pals asks which pet needs care. A lawn mower might ask which areas need mowing, and a car washer might ask about vehicle size. Keep children’s names, home access details and emergency arrangements for a private conversation.

Set the confirmation message to Thanks for getting in touch. We’ll review your enquiry and contact you to discuss the next step. Publish for anyone with the link. Leave Limit to 1 response and View results summary off. Use a normal question for contact details so a customer can respond without Google sign-in. Copy the responder link and test from a signed-out browser.

Use the form’s responder link for the QR code and print the link or include a parent-managed contact method as a fallback. Scan the printed flyer from another phone. Check it opens the enquiry form and asks for no account.

5. Practise one booking together

  1. Submit a fictional enquiry from a phone. Confirm it appears once in the spreadsheet.
  2. Create a booking reference and have the parent check the request. Practise agreeing the date, service and total before sending confirmation.
  3. At the first visit, agree what the job involves and who to call if something changes. The parent records the essentials privately. For Alice’s Pet Pals this includes care instructions. For another service, swap in the customer’s instructions for that job. The Website & Booking Kit uses a restricted phone form to send first-visit notes to the register.
  4. After the practice visit, mark the job complete. Practise recording payment and sending a brief update about the job.
  5. Ask your teen which part felt easy and which needed help before trying the first real booking.
